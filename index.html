Op-Amp Math

A 10000 Ω feedback over a 1000 Ω to ground gives a gain of 11. 0.2 V in comes out at 2.2 V.

Boundary: arithmetic only Op-Amp Math works out ideal op-amp gains and output voltages from the numbers you type. The resistances and voltages are yours to measure or read off your own circuit. It treats every op-amp as ideal, so it has not seen your circuit and does not know the supply rails, the bandwidth, the offset or the chip variant, and it gives no verdict that an amplifier circuit is adequate or safe. Every number here is your own entry.

Starting examples: 10000 Ω over 1000 Ω non-inverting, 10000 Ω over 2000 Ω inverting, and a two-input sum through 10000 Ω and 5000 Ω.

The non-inverting truth

A 10000 Ω feedback over a 1000 Ω to ground gives a gain of 11. 0.2 V in comes out at 2.2 V.

The inverting truth

With 10000 Ω feedback and 2000 Ω in, the gain is -5. 0.2 V in comes out at -1 V.

The summing truth

With 10000 Ω feedback, 0.5 V through 10000 Ω and 1 V through 5000 Ω sum to -2.5 V.
